Why it matters

Reels, WhatsApp, podcasts, influencer posts, sponsored content and AI-generated promotion are now named as media of advertisement. There is no transition period, and posts already live may be looked at. This 8-slide briefing is tap-through: swipe or use the buttons below.

Every post

The disclosure line

Each electronic-media post should show the doctor’s name, qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR number. Put it on the post itself, not only in your bio. On video, add it to an end card too.

Also: patient testimonials can’t be requested or shared for promotion on social media, and patient consent doesn’t fix that.

Still fine

What you can do

The guidelines don’t ban social media. Education and facts remain open.

  • Public-health education in your own name and designation, with no promotional intent
  • Talks on TV, radio and electronic media in your own name, without promoting your employer
  • Factual notices: starting practice, change of address, absence, fees
  • Case studies in reputed medical journals
  • Health-education leaflets for patients visiting your clinic
  • Greetings on national days and days important to the profession
  • Hospitals: factual, verifiable details of departments, equipment, emergency services, accreditation and charges
  • AI help for educational drafts, with a visible AI source mark
AI warning

AI-generated promotional campaigns are prohibited. No synthetic patients, voices, testimonials or doctor avatars.

Process

Show-cause notice first, then a reasoned order. First appeal to the NMC Ethics & Medical Registration Board within 60 days; a second appeal within 60 days of that decision. Using an agency or influencer doesn’t remove your responsibility for content you authorised.
